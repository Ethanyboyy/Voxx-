# The free audit — the thing that converts a maybe

This is the asset the whole offer rests on. You send it **free, filled in, with
no ask attached**, and it does the selling, because it is the first time anyone
has handed this person a specific written answer about their own business
instead of a sales call.

**Hard rule: never send one you have not checked.** Every line must be
something you actually looked at. One invented detail about someone's own
business ends the conversation and deserves to — they know their operation
better than any model does, and a confident wrong sentence tells them exactly
how much to trust the rest.

Keep it to one page. Two at most. A long audit reads as padding.

---

## Template

> **[Business name] — where the week is going**
> Prepared by [your name], [date]. Free, no strings.
>
> I had a look at how [business] takes bookings and bills, from the outside.
> Three things stood out. I may be wrong about any of them — you'd know.
>
> ---
>
> **1. [The specific thing]**
> What I see: [exactly what you observed, and where. "Your booking page asks
> for a phone number then tells people to call anyway."]
> What it probably costs: [a range, in hours or dollars per week, with the
> arithmetic shown. "If that's 6 calls a day at ~4 minutes, that's about 2
> hours a week."]
> What I'd do: [one concrete change, in one sentence.]
>
> **2. [The second thing]**
> *(same three lines)*
>
> **3. [The third thing]**
> *(same three lines)*
>
> ---
>
> **If you want the first one done:** I can build and hand it over inside 48
> hours for $450 flat. If it isn't working the way I've described, you don't
> pay. If you'd rather just do it yourself, items 1–3 above are the whole plan —
> genuinely, take them.
>
> [your name] · [phone/email]

## What makes these land

**Show the arithmetic.** "You're losing about 2 hours a week" is a claim.
"6 calls a day × 4 minutes ≈ 2 hours a week" is a calculation they can check
and argue with, which is what makes it credible.

**Give a range, never a point estimate.** "Somewhere between 2 and 5 hours"
survives being wrong. "4.2 hours" invites them to find the error and dismiss
the document.

**Mean the "take it yourself" line.** It is the reason the free audit works at
all, and some people will take it and not pay — that is the cost of the channel,
not a leak in it. The ones who do not want to do it themselves are the ones who
were always going to pay you.

**Lead with the thing they already know is broken.** You are not trying to
surprise them. You are trying to be the first person who wrote it down and
offered to fix it by Thursday.
